Personal information
In order to provide you products and services of interest to you as well as ongoing support, we collect certain personal information. This may be during the application process; when communicating with you in our stores, online and over the phone; and when you browse the Internet. We may also gather information about you from third parties, such as creditors or credit reporting agencies.
What is personal information?
Your personal information includes data that can identify you. Here are some examples:
Your name, address, email address and phone number
Information about the Bell products you subscribe to
Your service usage details, such as call records or data usage
Account information, such as your account status or method of payment
Health and biometric information, if provided
To protect your privacy, we remove details that can identify you personally whenever possible.
Learn about de-identifying data
Why do we gather personal information?
We collect personal information so we can:
Establish a business relationship with you, for example to confirm your identity
Ensure that your services are working correctly
Understand your needs and preferences to recommend products, services and discounts that might interest you
Understand how you use our products and services and how we can improve our offering
Manage and develop our business, for example, monitoring usage volumes to plan for our networks, and tracking product sales to measure their success
Meet legal and regulatory requirements, such as collecting information to comply with a CRTC regulation
Sharing your information
To get a better idea of your needs and how we can fulfill them, we sometimes share information between our companies.1 We only disclose personal information with parties outside our family of companies and suppliers when we are required to or when you have given express consent for us to do so.
These third parties are carefully selected partners acting on our behalf. We only disclose as much information as they need for a specific purpose, such as shipping you a new phone or providing network service while you’re roaming. When using your personal information, we require the companies and their employees to uphold our privacy standards. See our Privacy Policy (PDF) for more information.
If our partners store or process your personal information outside Canada, they are legally bound to protect your data using strict privacy safeguards.
1-“Our companies” refers to the following organizations and brands:
AAA Alarm Systems Ltd., Alarm Force Industries Inc. (Bell Smart Home, Bell Business Security and Automation), Axia, Bell Aliant, Bell Canada, Bell ExpressVu LP (Bell TV), Bell Media, Bell Mobility, Bell MTS, Cablevision du Nord, DMTS, Epic Communications, KMTS, Lucky Mobile, Maskatel, NorthernTel, Northwestel Inc., Ontera, Solo Mobile, Télébec and Virgin Plus.

Cookies
Cookies are small text files that your web browser creates as you use the Internet. The information they store does not personally identify who you are, but does allow a website to remember your actions or preferences – for instance, remembering the items you added to your shopping cart, or the preference you set to stay logged in to your account.
Information from cookies helps us provide a better web experience by telling us about the people who visit our sites and how they use them, such as:
How often our websites are visited, from where and on what device or browser – this helps us ensure you can access our sites when and how you want to, on your preferred device
What content on a site is popular and how visitors interact with it – knowing what is clicked on or shared helps us offer you useful information up front
While information we gather with cookies does not personally identify you, with your permission we are able to identify you if you sign in to one of our websites or apps.
Cookies are also used by our advertising partners to display ads that are relevant to you. For example, after visiting a Bell website, you may see an ad for Bell while browsing another website.
You can always disable cookies in your browser settings.

De-identifying data
De-identifying data means removing information that could link the data back to you as an individual. If personal information is properly de-identified so that it can no longer be linked to you, the information is no longer considered personal.
To protect your privacy, we de-identify data where possible. We do this by:
Removing details such as your name, government identification numbers (e.g. driver’s licence number) and other direct identifiers, as well as data that can identify you indirectly, such as your date of birth or gender
Transforming details so they no longer identify you personally, such as storing just the first three letters rather than your full postal code, or using an age range rather than your date of birth

Analytics and artificial intelligence
Data analytics is the science of looking closely at data, uncovering information and drawing conclusions based on that information. We analyze customer data for a variety of reasons, such as to improve customer service, efficiency, safety and security, as well as to protect intellectual property.
There are four main types of data analytics:
Descriptive analytics shows what happened, such as a spike in TV viewership during a popular sports event.
Diagnostic analytics explains why something happened, for example, that a mobile phone outage was caused by a sudden surge in usage.
Predictive analytics helps us foresee what might happen in the future based on past events. For example, predicting how many extra calls our customer service might get when a new service launches.
Prescriptive analytics helps us identify a solution to a problem. For instance, understanding day-to-day Internet usage, unusual events and predicted future usage patterns might point to ways we can improve our network.
Artificial intelligence
Artificial intelligence (AI) techniques accelerate decision-making processes by analyzing enormous amounts of data very quickly. This helps us improve your experience, as well as our products and services.
For example, AI techniques might detect more calls than usual coming from a particular neighbourhood, recognize that a new service just launched there and automatically direct callers from that area to an agent with the right skills.
We always review the decisions made by AI to ensure they are fair, lawful, and do not have unintended consequences.

Utiq technology
What is the Utiq service?
Utiq is a global advertising technology company that started in Europe. It enables advertising and personalized content when you visit third-party websites that integrate the Utiq technology with the goal of responsible digital marketing.
At Bell, we are committed to privacy and believe that our customers have the right to choose and participate in having control over their privacy. With this shared goal in mind, you are only participating in the Utiq program if you provide your express consent.
Bell as a signal provider
Bell, as your telecom operator, will provide Utiq with an online advertising identifier after you have given such express consent.
The Utiq technology is off by default. It can only be activated when you use a supported Internet connection and give your consent to its activation on the digital properties (such as websites or apps) that make use of it.
When you opt in, Bell as your telecom operator provides Utiq with a pseudonymous online advertising identifier which is generated using your IP address and a business reference such as your telecom account number or Mobility subscription with us. Only the identifier is shared with Utiq, not the underlying details. Utiq then uses this to generate additional marketing identifiers for publishers.
Utiq provides you with a privacy portal (“consenthub”) to facilitate the exercise of your data protection rights and enable you to easily manage any Utiq consents given at any time.
You can find all the relevant information about the Utiq technology in Utiq’s Privacy Statement. You can learn about how we work with Utiq, which Internet connections are supported and what personal data we process in this page about telecom operators' involvement.
Bell as a publisher/website
Bell Media may use the Utiq technology on its websites and apps to personalize content and advertising. When you consent to Utiq, it provides Bell Media brands with unique identifiers that help us understand how you interact with our content. This allows us to tailor your experience and show you more personalized content and ads. For example, if you consent to Utiq on a brand website and show interest in sports, we may highlight sports content for you.
Bell Media may also share these identifiers within our advertising framework (with your consent) to enable targeted advertising across its different brands. If you provide consent to Utiq on multiple Bell Media brands, we can gain insights across those brands. For example, we may discern that users on our entertainment sites have a tendency to also enjoy our home and garden content. These identifiers are not shared with non-Bell brands.
It's important to note that Utiq may be linked to your Internet connection, so other users on the same Wi-Fi network may share the same identifier (e.g., you and other members in your household may see ads based on each other’s interests). You can control your Utiq settings and learn more about our data practices through the "Manage Utiq" page where available or through Utiq’s consenthub.

Advertising IDs
An advertising ID is a unique identifier generated from your email address or phone number, converted into a random sequence of numbers and letters to protect your privacy. These IDs exist for only a few days before being deleted. They are de-identified and not linked to your name or other personal data we collect from you.
We use advertising IDs to:
deliver personalized offers and relevant advertising to customers
measure advertising effectiveness at an aggregate level for advertising partners
understand customers interests and actions on Bell websites, apps and services where customers have given their consent
One of the ways we use advertising IDs is in a “clean room” setting. A clean room is a secure digital environment that enables multiple parties to share and analyze data while protecting individual privacy and ensuring compliance with privacy laws and regulations.
A clean room entity will match an advertising ID with non-Bell identifiers (based on personal information you may have provided to other companies you have a relationship with). Advertising IDs can also be combined with advertising exposure data to create aggregated reporting that measure advertising effectiveness and customer overlap. Advertising partners do not learn the identity of our customers in this process.
You have the Choice to Opt-Out
You have the choice to opt-out of these advertising IDs. You can email us at privacy@bell.ca with the subject line: “Advertising IDs Opt-out”
