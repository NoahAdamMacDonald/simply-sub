What Is a No-Logs VPN?
A no-logs VPN doesn’t keep records of your browsing while you’re connected to its servers. This includes your online history, searches, downloads, or the apps and websites you access during a session. A true no-logs VPN also limits the technical data it collects, avoiding information that could later be used to link browsing back to you.
Some VPNs simply claim they don’t keep logs, while others invite independent security firms to audit their systems and verify their no-logs claims. Reputable VPNs often also choose to operate from privacy-friendly jurisdictions without mandatory data-retention laws to ensure they don’t have to legally store and share user data.

How Does a No-Logs VPN Work?
When you connect to a VPN, it sends your internet traffic through a VPN server before it reaches websites or apps. The VPN encrypts your connection and replaces your real IP address with the server’s IP, which makes it harder to link activity back to your device.
A no-logs VPN takes this further, as it configures its infrastructure not to store records of your browsing. Providers avoid saving information such as browsing history, connection timestamps, or session details that could later be tied to a specific user.
Since a no-logs VPN doesn’t store these records, it has nothing to share or hand over to anyone requesting it, like advertisers.

Types of VPN Logs
Activity or usage logs: Record what you do online while connected to a VPN. They can include browsing history, websites visited, downloads, or apps used. They reveal direct details about your activity, so privacy-focused VPNs typically avoid collecting them.
Connection logs: Track technical details about your VPN sessions. This may include connection times, session duration, or the IP address used to connect. Providers sometimes collect limited connection data to monitor performance or troubleshoot network issues.
Aggregated logs: Collect general statistics about network usage rather than individual activity. This can include overall bandwidth usage, server load, or connection counts. VPNs usually combine this data across users so it can’t be linked to a specific person.

FAQ
What does “no logs VPN” actually mean?
A no-logs VPN is a service that doesn’t store records of your online activity. That means it doesn’t keep search history, connection timestamps, or other data that could link browsing back to you. CyberGhost VPN enforces this policy using RAM-only servers that automatically wipe session data on every reboot, so nothing is written to a hard drive.
How can I verify CyberGhost VPN’s no-logs policy?
CyberGhost VPN's no-logs policy has already been verified through independent audits. We regularly invite reputable firms, including Deloitte, to review our systems and confirm that we follow our privacy commitments. These audits provide external verification that our infrastructure operates as described.
What kind of data do other VPNs track?
It depends on the provider. While reputable VPNs aim to collect as little data as possible, some providers collect so much connection metadata that it’s relatively easy to link it to your browsing activity. Some even sell this information to third parties.
Can you get fined for using a VPN?
In most countries, you can use a VPN without getting fined. However, some governments restrict or regulate VPN use, Always check local laws and regulations before using a VPN, and remember that anything illegal without a VPN remains illegal when you use one.
Do VPN companies sell your data?
That depends on which VPN you choose. CyberGhost VPN is a strictly no-logs VPN, so we don’t track or share details about your session. We couldn’t sell your data because we don’t log any. But the same can’t be said for all VPNs. Some free VPNs rely on advertising or data collection to generate revenue, so it’s important to choose a VPN with a no-logs policy.
Do all VPNs hide your location?
Yes, a reliable VPN can help hide your location from third parties. CyberGhost VPN masks your IP address with one from its server, so the websites you visit only see the VPN’s IP address, not yours. However, some VPNs use weak security features that make it easy for your real location to leak.
Can an email be traced if I use a VPN?
A VPN changes the IP address associated with your connection, so emails sent while connected may appear to come from the VPN server rather than your home network. However, email providers and other metadata can still identify your accounts or devices, so a VPN doesn’t make email completely anonymous.
Who can see my browsing history with a VPN?
A VPN encrypts your traffic, which helps prevent local networks, ISPs, or Wi-Fi administrators from easily seeing the websites you visit. However, the websites themselves can still see your activity, and services you log into can track your behavior within their platforms.
Can my company track my VPN?
If you’re using a company-provided VPN or work device, your employer may still monitor activity through corporate systems or device management tools. Using a personal VPN service is different, but workplace policies and monitoring software can still apply.
Who can see my VPN data?
When you use a VPN, your ISP or local network can usually see that you’re connected to a VPN, but not the contents of your encrypted traffic. The VPN provider handles your connection, which is why choosing a trustworthy provider with strong privacy practices is important.
Can someone see my internet history if I use their Wi-Fi with a VPN?
When you connect through a VPN on someone else’s Wi-Fi network, the network owner can usually see that your device is connected and that you’re using a VPN. However, the websites you visit and the contents of your traffic are encrypted and harder for others on that network to view.
