Dashlane security FAQs
How does Dashlane protect your data?
Dashlane uses a zero-knowledge security architecture, which means no one, including Dashlane, has access to your data. Your logins and personal information are always encrypted, even when we store your data on our servers as a backup or to sync it across your devices. When you enter your Master Password, your data is decrypted on your device and available only to you. Dashlane also uses Dark Web Monitoring to alert you if any of your personal info shows up in a data breach, so that you can take action quickly.
How does Dashlane operate securely?
Beyond the core model, Dashlane maintains continuous vulnerability scanning and patching, a decade-long HackerOne bug bounty program, independent annual penetration testing across all apps and cloud infrastructure, and 24/7 incident monitoring backed by a tested response plan. Results are shared publicly through the Dashlane Trust Center.
How does Dashlane prevent unauthorized access to corporate vaults?
Vault data is encrypted on your device before it reaches Dashlane's servers, so even a compromised server should not expose credentials. When you log in on a new device, that device generates a unique 40-byte key that requires explicit verification—separate from the vault encryption key—so only trusted endpoints can decrypt vault data. Admins enforce access controls with role-based permissions in the Admin Console.
What role does confidential computing play in Dashlane’s enterprise security?
AWS Nitro Enclaves extend Dashlane's zero-knowledge protection to the cloud with hardware-level isolation for sensitive cryptographic operations. Enclaves keep those operations invisible to the host system—including Dashlane itself. For enterprise features like single sign-on (SSO) and SCIM provisioning, zero-knowledge guarantees hold at every level of the authentication workflow.
How does Dashlane secure IdP integrations for SSO and SCIM provisioning?
Dashlane handles SSO and SCIM operations inside AWS Nitro Enclaves, so cryptographic operations happen in a hardware-isolated environment with no visibility from the host system. Zero-knowledge guarantees hold throughout provisioning, deprovisioning, and authentication workflows. Dashlane integrates with most SAML 2.0 identity providers.
What happens if Dashlane's servers are breached?
A breach of Dashlane's servers would only expose encrypted data. All vault data is encrypted locally on your device with AES-256 encryption before it reaches Dashlane's servers, and the keys needed to decrypt it never leave your possession.
How does Dashlane's zero-knowledge architecture compare to other password managers?
Zero-knowledge encryption of vault data is a standard Dashlane shares with other leading password managers: your data is encrypted on your device, and only you hold the keys. Where Dashlane goes further in the cloud: AWS Nitro Enclaves extend zero-knowledge guarantees to enterprise workflows, such as SSO authentication and SCIM provisioning. Dashlane also ensures all activity logs for admins are encrypted end-to-end and zero-knowledge. Dashlane holds 10 patents for this approach to zero-knowledge security, with 5 more pending.
Does Dashlane's zero-knowledge architecture support compliance requirements?
Yes. Because Dashlane cannot access or decrypt your data, zero-knowledge architecture reduces the data exposure risk that compliance frameworks address. Dashlane is SOC 2 Type II certified and ISO 27001 certified. Encrypted activity logs and role-based access controls support compliance reporting. Visit the Dashlane Trust Center for current certifications and full documentation.
