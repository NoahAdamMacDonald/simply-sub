Netflix Bug Bounty Program Policy
Netflix has been engaging with the security research community for years to help keep our members, partners, employees, data and content secure. Keep in mind, the Netflix Bug Bounty Scope is broader than just the streaming targets: member-facing web and mobile apps, content authorization, and our Enterprise/Corporate systems are also in scope too. We run under HackerOne's Gold Standard Safe Harbor and follow HackerOne's Platform Standards.
We've paid out for subdomain takeovers, cache poisoning on prod hosts, request smuggling on one of our services, and plenty more...
what's in scope, what actually pays, what's valid but won't earn a bounty, and what we've already seen so you don't burn time rediscovering it.

Why Hunt Here
There's no fixed price tag per bug class. We look at the total impact of the report to Netflix, and pay accordingly based on the target type. Two reports in the same category can land differently based on the impact to Netflix.
A few real numbers, pulled from reports we've closed and paid:
$25,000 for one authentication/access control finding
$10,000 for cache poisoning on a high impact host.
$5,500 for request/response smuggling through a critical service.
$5,000 each for subdomain takeover and for path traversal on Critical services parsing logic.
$4,000 for a signal handler race condition in a native component.

Not sure where you land? First confirm the target is actually Netflix owned, then skim the Focus Areas, Out of Scope, and Excluded Submission Types sections.
Still unsure but you think it's worth flagging? Submit through the program and explain your thinking. If we decide to not act on it, that's a good faith submission closed as Informative, not Not Applicable. No points penalty either way.
Validate Before Submission
Save yourself some time and check a few things before you write anything up:
Is it a Focus Area? If not, make the case for why it's impactful to Netflix. Netflix will evaluate the report on a case by case basis.
Does it hit Excluded Submission Types or Out of Scope? Check against those lists before you write the report.
Have we seen it? If it matches a Known Issue we already track as a duplicate or an accepted, capped risk, it'll probably land the same way again, unless you can show an impact that's genuinely different from what's already on file.
Exploring on these three sections will help you focus on finding and reporting novel and unique findings.
Known Issues
Vulnerability reports that duplicate an issue we have already received, triaged, and are actively tracking will be closed as Duplicate. Below are the vulnerability classes we most frequently receive as repeat submissions, grouped by root cause. If your finding matches one of these patterns, it is very likely already known to us, so please check carefully before submitting.
Household Verification Check (Mobile & Web). The Household verification enforcement is actively being worked on and improved. We are aware of certain bypasses that team is actively working to mitigate and fix.
Credentialed CORS Misconfiguration. Several internal hosts show a behaviour of reflecting an arbitrary request Origin in Access Control Allow Origin while also allowing credentials. These are not exploitable due to internal control as a defense in depth
Profile & Session Pin Controls. Profile PIN enforcement issues are a known, actively tracked class. Also tracked: a profile creation race condition around the per account profile limit, and missing PIN verification rate limiting.
Publicly Exposed S3 Assets & Hardcoded DRM Keys. Publicly listable/exposed S3 assets under download.opencontent.netflix.com, including reports identifying hardcoded DRM keys.
Hardcoded Credentials in Public Sample/Test Code. "Sample TEST Private key / truststore password" committed in the public Zuul sample/test code repository.
Public OIDC Discovery Endpoint. OIDC discovery/JWKS endpoints publicly reachable.
Safe Harbor
We follow HackerOne's Gold Standard Safe Harbor. To encourage responsible disclosure, Netflix will not bring a lawsuit against you or ask law enforcement to investigate you if we determine that your research and disclosure meet these requirements and guidelines. If you have questions about responsible disclosure of results for a submission, please reach out to us via the submission page
Disclosure Stance
This program or engagement does not allow disclosure. You may not release information about vulnerabilities found in this program or engagement to the public.
Program Guidelines
Here's what we expect from every researcher:
Test only within the Scope defined below.
Do NOT access Customer and employee personal data, pre-release content, and Netflix confidential material. If you accidentally gain access to any of it, stop testing immediately and report it.
Please make sure your submission includes a verified, reproducible proof of concept. AI-generated or low-effort reports without confirmed reproduction steps will be closed without review.
If you land in a non-public app or obtain non-public credentials, stop and report it right away.
Don't degrade the experience for real users, disrupt production, or destroy data while you're testing.
Grab only what you need to prove the vulnerability, and send it through the HackerOne report form (screenshots, recordings, requests, repro steps, POC). Do NOT use third-party file-sharing sites.
When investigating a vulnerability, please only target your own account and do not attempt to access data from anyone else's account.
Submit through HackerOne only.
Note: Do NOT access personal data, confidential data, or pre-release content , and if you accidentally gain access, stop and immediately report it. If you land on a private credential or an internal/private application or data, stop and report it to us immediately.
Data Deletion
Once you've submitted your report, securely delete any Netflix data you downloaded, cached, or otherwise kept on your research systems.
The same goes for anything you stored in third-party tools or services while testing. Delete that too.
Securely delete Netflix information that may have been downloaded, cached, or otherwise stored on the systems used to perform the research
What Netflix Will Do
When working on bug bounty submissions, here are our commitments to you:
We'll work with you to understand the issue and try to fix it quickly.
We'll confirm your report within 7 days.
If you're the first to report a unique, in-scope vulnerability through HackerOne, you will receive a reward (see Rewards under each tier below) after the submission has gone through our process.
We'll hold up our end of Safe Harbor.
If the same bug shows up across multiple parts of the app or view (e.g., the same injection/XSS pattern hitting several endpoints), we may pay extra on top of the ranges below, as long as your report clearly spells out each distinct vector.

Corporate Targets
In scope: How Netflix uses and configures G Suite for netflix.com, netflixcontractors.com, and netflixanimation.com, not any subdomain of netflix.com, and not unrelated domains like netflixcs.com. We're interested in reports related to Netflix controlled configuration bypasses, not bugs in G Suite itself. We value knowing the root cause, not just what you saw.
Individual Google documents that are publicly exposed can create risk, but we value root causes over reports of individual documents. Maximum payouts will come from reporting addressable root causes, not individual documents.
Multiple documents that share a common root cause for their exposure will be paid out on the appropriate Target matrix based on the change necessary to address the root cause (payout amount may be higher or lower than simply based on the sensitivity & the number of exposed documents). Individual documents with no common root cause typically will be low severity under "Corporate Targets". We will reward only where we choose to make a change.
For documents that contain particularly sensitive information (typically personal information or active credentials), we may choose a higher priority and associated payout range, based on impact. We expect this to be rare and on a case-by-case basis, and this determination is made by Netflix.
Note: if an exposed doc turns out to contain customer or employee personal info, or Netflix confidential material, submit right away.
Rewards:
Severity Minimum Maximum
Critical $2,000 $10,000
High $500 $2,000
Critical and High only (by CVSS). Medium and Low Corporate reports are accepted but non-rewardable.
Content Authorization & Device Targets
In scope: Getting playback on a device that shouldn't have it, whether by bypassing authorization checks or getting your hands on private keys that back the system.
Video decryption keys are in scope, but the report needs to include actual working key material at the time you report it (a rotated or dead key doesn't count), plus specific, actionable details on how you discovered or extracted it: that's required to qualify for a reward. Submissions related to screenshots or screen recording are not in scope; you must include the actual key material.
Keys leaked together, or pulled the same way across multiple devices, count as one finding, not one reward per key. Pulling a key from hardware (a TEE, say) pays more than pulling one from software, because it's a lot harder to do.
Bypasses that only work in the browser player are Non Rewardable: Kudos only. Already publicly broken protocols like HDCP v1.4 don't count; see Excluded Submission Types below.
Rewards:
Severity Minimum Maximum
High $1,000 $5,000
Medium $300 $1,000
There is no Critical tier for this category. Low-severity findings are accepted but Non-Rewardable (Kudos only, no bounty).
Open Source Targets
Only specific Netflix open source projects are in scope, paid on the Primary or Secondary scale as noted:
Project Tier Repository
Zuul Primary Target github.com/Netflix/zuul
Atlas Secondary Target github.com/Netflix/atlas
Spectator Secondary Target github.com/Netflix/spectator
MSL Protocol is its own named Focus Area (see above), with its own repo at github.com/Netflix/msl. It's not in this Primary/Secondary table because MSL findings are prioritized by impact, not by a fixed tier.
Every other Netflix open-source project, including Dispatch, Weep, and ConsoleMe, is entirely out of scope here. Check each project's README / SECURITY file before testing; that's where its own scope and process live.
OSS Reports: Researchers must submit a working proof-of-concept script demonstrating the reported issue. Only the current major version and the immediately preceding major version of each in-scope OSS project are eligible; reports affecting older, unsupported major versions are closed as Out of Scope.
Non Rewardable Targets (In Scope, Kudos Only)
We still want to hear about these, and we'll award with Kudos points, but they aren't eligible for a bounty:
Affiliates or recently acquired entities.
Content Authorization bypasses that only work in the browser player.
A one off exposed Google Doc/Drive link with no shared root cause and low impact.
Netflix Gaming targets.
Special Case: Dependency Confusion via Package Takeover
We know about dependency confusion issues tied to package manager ownership and we're fixing it long term. Until then:
Same root cause as something already submitted? Closed as a Duplicate of the original.
Got evidence that a takeover package is actually running on or affecting Netflix infrastructure? Submit it; we want to know.
Show us something new or a way around our existing controls, and you'll get paid even if the general category has been reported before.
Out of Scope
These aren't submittable findings here: they get closed Not Applicable.
Third-party sites or systems we don't host (including unauthorized third-party microsites; see Secondary Targets above).
Investor relations sites: ir.netflix.net, ir.netflix.com, netflixinvestor.com.
Set top boxes, smart TVs, and streaming sticks.
Open Source repos other than Zuul, Atlas, and Spectator (see Open Source Targets above), including Dispatch, Weep, and ConsoleMe, which people sometimes assume are in scope because they're ours too.
ReadyPlayerMe assets.
Vendor owned and operated assets.
Anything on the Excluded Submission Types list right below, plus HackerOne's Core Ineligible Findings.

Reports relating to Symantec root certificates
Reports based on the reported version numbers of web servers, services, or frameworks alone

Program Discretion
We reserve the right to reward a report even if the target isn't explicitly listed. On rare occasions, we'll pay for an especially impactful, informative submission against an otherwise non-reward eligible target, but the types above are where the real money is. Keep in mind the bottom line: whether to pay a reward, and in what amount, is at Netflix's sole and exclusive discretion.
